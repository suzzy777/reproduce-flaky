ISSTA 2026 Paper #1117 Reviews and Comments
===========================================================================
Paper #1117 A Dataset of Reproducible Flaky-Test Failures


Review #1117A
===========================================================================

Overall merit
-------------
3. Weak accept

Paper summary
-------------
This paper presents a dataset of reproducible flaky tests,
complete with Docker images that enable researchers to
easily reproduce the flaky tests. The paper also offers a
characterization of flaky tests and suggestions about open
research problems that are surfaced by using the dataset.

Strengths
---------
- The paper presents a reproducible dataset with Docker images,
  which addresses a significant need in the flaky test research
  community for easily accessible and reproducible artifacts.
- The dataset includes both flaky and fixed versions of tests,
  enabling researchers to study the differences between flaky
  and non-flaky test behaviors.
- The paper provides a characterization of flaky tests that
  could serve as a starting point for future research.
- The dataset construction methodology is documented with
  sufficient detail to enable future extensions.

Weaknesses
----------
- The final research question (RQ3) is not clearly a research
  question and appears more as a documentation question about
  how to use the dataset, which weakens the paper's overall
  research contribution.
- The paper does not adequately demonstrate how the dataset
  enables researchers to draw more meaningful conclusions than
  prior datasets would allow, limiting understanding of its
  unique value proposition as a research enabler.
- The paper lacks concrete examples of follow-on research that
  leverages the dataset to show its practical utility.
- The use of Docker may limit detection of certain types of
  flakiness related to platform-specific behaviors, and this
  limitation is not sufficiently discussed.

Detailed comments for authors
-----------------------------
## Paper Evaluation

### Research Innovation

- **Description**: Describe innovative research in testing
  or analysis of computer software. Papers should describe
  original theoretical or empirical research, new techniques,
  methods for emerging systems, in-depth case studies,
  infrastructures of testing and analysis.

- **Evaluation**: This paper has acceptable research
  innovation. The creation of a reproducible dataset with
  Docker images addresses a real need in the flaky test
  community. However, the work would be stronger if it
  demonstrated more concrete research applications enabled
  by the dataset rather than just presenting RQ3 as a
  documentation question.

### Verifiability and Transparency

- **Description**: The extent to which the paper includes
  sufficient information to understand how the evidence for
  key claims in the paper were produced, and how the paper
  supports independent verification or replication of the
  paper’s claimed contributions. Any artifacts attached to or
  linked from the paper may be checked by one reviewer.

- **Evaluation**: This paper has good verifiability and
  transparency. The use of Docker containers and the detailed
  documentation of the dataset construction methodology
  support independent verification. The paper provides
  sufficient information to understand how the dataset was
  built and there is a replication package available.

### Presentation

- **Description**: The extent to which the paper's quality
  of writing meets the high standards of ISSTA, including
  clear descriptions, adequate use of the English language,
  absence of major ambiguity, clearly readable figures and
  tables, and adherence to the formatting instructions
  provided below.

- **Evaluation**: This paper has good presentation quality.
  The writing is clear and well-organized, with helpful tables
  comparing the dataset to prior work. The paper effectively
  communicates its contributions and methodology. Tables and
  figures in the paper are interesting and informative.

### Importance

- **Description**: The extent to which the paper's contributions
  can impact the field of software engineering in practice or
  in research, and under which assumptions (if any).

- **Evaluation**: This paper has acceptable importance.
  Reproducible datasets are valuable for advancing flaky test
  research. However, the paper would be stronger if it better
  demonstrated concrete ways in which the dataset enables
  research that prior datasets could not support.

### Originality

- **Description**: The extent to which the contributions are
  sufficiently original with respect to the state-of-the-art,
  including appropriate comparison to related work.

- **Evaluation**: This paper has acceptable originality.
  While prior flaky test datasets exist, the combination
  of reproducible Docker environments with paired flaky
  and fixed versions represents a novel contribution.

### Soundness

- **Description**: The extent to which the paper's key claims are
  supported by rigorous application of appropriate research
  methods, including sound methods for empirical evaluation.

- **Evaluation**: The dataset construction methodology is
  well-documented and appears rigorous. However, the framing

      easily access this dataset and preserve my anonymity

      making it more clear how reviewers can access the
      dataset while preserving their anonymity.

      research challenges, I judge that the paper would be
      stronger if it concluded with a summary of the most
      important future work that this paper enables.
    - Finally, one area that it would have been great to see
      the paper develop further is actual follow-on work
      that leverages the data set. For instance, is there an
      existing flaky test detection technique that you could
      evaluate with a different dataset and then with your
      presented data set? Can you show that without your
      dataset a researcher would arrive at misleading
      conclusions about the method's effectiveness?

Questions for authors' response
-------------------------------
- **Q1**: How does the ReproFlake dataset enable researchers
  to draw more meaningful conclusions than prior flaky test
  datasets? Can you provide concrete examples of research
  questions that could not be adequately addressed with prior
  datasets but can be with yours?
- **Q2**: Why is RQ3 framed as a research question rather
  than documentation? What research insight does this question
  advance, and how does it contribute to understanding the
  dataset or flaky tests more broadly?
- **Q3**: Can you demonstrate the practical utility of the
  dataset by evaluating an existing flaky testing technique
  using both a prior dataset and ReproFlake, showing that
  conclusions differ or that your dataset provides value?



Review #1117B
===========================================================================

Overall merit
-------------
2. Weak reject

Paper summary
-------------
This paper presents a new dataset of reproducible Java flaky tests, ReproFlake. The data is gathered from Jira issues and iDoFT (a dataset of flaky tests collected in prior research). The paper details the process to construct the dataset, which contains in the 1,115 reproducible flaky unit tests across the categories ID, TD, OD, NIO, and unclassified. In addition, the papers provide some analysis of the collected data, including the challenges with reproducing flaky test failures, the characteristics of flaky tests, and difficulty to add new data, i.e., code coverage. When reporting the results of the empirical analysis, the authors also present multiple "Open Research Challenge" that can drive further research.

Strengths
---------
- The research topic is relevant: reproducible flaky tests

Weaknesses
----------
- Dataset is the main contribution
- Lack of motivation for the RQs

Detailed comments for authors
-----------------------------
The research topic (flaky tests) is relevant and it is in the context of ISSTA. Overall, the paper is well-written and easy to follow. The authors tackle an important problem that is having access to reproducible flaky tests and a reproducible environment, which is not provided by any prior dataset, such as iDoFT and FlakeFlagger. One potential limitation is regarding the fact the main contribution of the paper is the dataset itself (which comes partially from iDoFT). While the dataset allows novel studies to be performed on top, the paper does not mainly report a theoretical, empirical, conceptual, or experimental research on software testing and analysis. 

It would be nice if the authors could present more examples of flaky tests, as presented in Section 3 (apache commons-collections). From the reader's perspective it would be interesting to have at least one example per test type (ID, TD, OD, and NIO).

Research questions are introduced in Section 5 without proper motivation and rationales. It is unclear the rationales to conduct the three research questions. While I understand the importance of each RQ, the authors should make it clear in the paper why exactly they are investigating: challenges, characteristics, and difficulty.

I found RQ3 to be very simple. The authors argue that the goal is to verify the "Process for Extending the Dataset with Additional Tools", but focus solely on code coverage (JaCoCo). Thus, the RQ could be rephrased to something like "Process for Extending the Dataset with Code Coverage". Are there other tools of interest (e.g., mutation)? Please, provide more details on how other tools could be incorporated (or tone it down to the context coverage).

Section 4.2.1-4 (Jira/Reproduction) describes 3 out 4 flaky test types (TD, OD, and ID), but it seems to miss NIO. How are Non-Idempotent-Outcome (NIO) tests detected? It seems that they all come from the iDoFT Dataset. Please, make this clearer in Section 4.2.1-4.

Please, make it clear in the abstract that the dataset is focused on Java.

Data availability: The paper provides a link to a webpage (https://sites.google.com/view/reproflakedataset), which contains multiple pointers, for data (https://doi.org/10.5281/zenodo.17508466), code (https://drive.google.com/drive/folders/1lMsreaL9QGuII3rEWSc4D4A3wFv-iAZy), infrastructure (https://drive.google.com/drive/folders/1Gf3mcsBPJAkvbB_QRD4nU92HjiZ3pi-b?usp=drive_link), and logs (https://drive.google.com/file/d/1SC7MHgfbmUrP096nUipOB7K4xRpwYHnE/view?usp=sharing).

Importance: Overall, the research topic is interesting and relevant. The authors tackle an important problem that is having access to reproducible flaky tests and a reproducible environment, which is not provided by any prior dataset.

Originality: The study is reasonably original as the main contribution is the dataset itself, which comes partially from iDoFT.

Soundness: The dataset construction and the generated artifacts are well-described. RQs could be better motivated.

Verifiability and Transparency: the paper provides a link to a webpage (https://sites.google.com/view/reproflakedataset), which contains multiple pointers, for data, code, infrastructure, and logs.

Presentation: Text and tables have a good quality, including clear descriptions, adequate use of the English language and absence of major ambiguity.

Review #1117C
===========================================================================

Overall merit
-------------
3. Weak accept

Paper summary
-------------
The paper reports on a newly constructed dataset of flaky tests. The key feature of this new dataset is to make it easily reproducible through automated scripts, include patches that witness the fixed issues and includes related issue reports. The paper results in a set of guidelines that aim at helping users to reproduce flaky tests as well as a set of several characteristics/challenges related to the practical flaky test reproduction and repair.

Strengths
---------
- Important topic
- Builds a reproducible dataset containing 1,115 flaky tests
- Identifies challenges of the practical flaky test reproduction and repair

Weaknesses
----------
- Research findings are a bit weak 
- Limitations of the dataset are not clearly stated

Detailed comments for authors
-----------------------------
The paper deals with an important research problem, the reproducibility of the test subjects used by many researchers. To this end, the paper reports on the creation of a flaky test dataset that required significant labor-intensive collection process. To this end, the dataset is accompanied by several scripts that automate the compilation, execution and patching of the flaky tests used. 

I like the entire endeavor and consider it important for the support of that particular research area. The paper is also well-written and well presented and therefore I do not have major comments or concerns about the paper/work. 

One point that is not really discussed in the paper, is the fact that many flaky tests originate from the test/production environment/infrastructure used. These seem to be hard to reproduce in a general way limiting the data included in the dataset. I do understand the need for reproducibility but what happens with the excluded cases? More generally, the limitations of the dataset are not clearly described in the paper (sorry if I missed them). This is important as datasets typically are used by research communities to form and compare most of the developed approaches. This makes important the knowledge of the related limitations and potential omissions.

To this end, do you have any insights on why many flaky tests (reported by users) are non-reproducible? Are these non-flaky in the general case or are non-flaky in the particular reproduction enviroment? 

Another point is the inclusion of only flaky tests. At least by reading the paper this is the impression I got. Much of the research in this area is at detecting flaky test, in some sense distinguishing flaky from non-flaky tests. Therefore, including non-flaky tests, or at least likely non-flaky tests, would be good. If the inclusion of all tests is too much of an effort, the dataset could include a set of similar tests to enable such research. 

Overall, the paper is interesting. Though, it feels more like a dataset paper rather than a full research paper as the key research findings are somehow expected and relative weak.

Questions for authors' response
-------------------------------
1. Why not including non-flaky tests in the dataset?
